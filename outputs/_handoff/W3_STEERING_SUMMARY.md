# W3 causal steering — cross-model verdict

Pre-registration: `W3_PRESPEC.md` (predictions P1-P4 written before the first run; amended 2026-07-28 to fix a broken coherence detector, thresholds unchanged). Per-model detail: `W3_STEERING_<model>.md`. Generated by `code/experiments/50_w3_summary.py`.

## Headline

**The pre-specified prediction failed, and it failed informatively.** In neither model does steering the intent direction move the moral contrast more than the outcome-direction control, and the intent direction fitted from probe weights — the very vector whose decoding accuracy is the project's representational evidence — moves the contrast by at most 0.016 at any coherent coefficient. The method is not insensitive: the outcome direction moves the same contrast by up to 0.26 in the same models, at the same coefficients, with the model still fluent. So the intervention works; the intent direction simply does not drive the judgment.

## Why the diff-of-means effect is not evidence of intent steering

Where the intent diff-of-means direction does move the contrast, it moves **all four cells upward at once**. The accidental cell already sits near the top of the scale, so it has less headroom than the attempted cell; a uniform upward push therefore shrinks the negative attempted-accidental gap automatically. That is compression against the ceiling, not a change in how intent is weighted.

## Pre-specified predictions, evaluated

- **P1 (direction specificity): NOT SUPPORTED in 2 of 2 models.** The intent direction never exceeds the outcome control. In OLMo-2 it does not even exceed a matched-norm *random* direction (0.081 vs 0.124).
- **P2 (dose-response): inconsistent.** OLMo-2-1124-7B-Instruct monotone; Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct non-monotone. A dose-response curve that appears in one model and not the other cannot carry a causal claim on its own.
- **P3 (method agreement): FAILED in both models, and this is the decisive result.** OLMo-2-1124-7B-Instruct cos=0.5142, |Δ| 0.081 vs 0.013; Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct cos=0.3537, |Δ| 0.225 vs 0.015. The two estimators of the *same* construct disagree by more than an order of magnitude in effect. The pre-registration states the consequence explicitly: if they disagree, the effect is a property of one estimator, not of the representation, and P1 is not supported.
- **P4 (coherence): satisfied, and not the reason P1 failed.** Perplexity stayed within 1.5x, refusal rate did not rise, task compliance stayed at 1.00, and a manual read at the largest coherent coefficient shows the models still summarising the stories accurately. The null is measured where the models are demonstrably intact, so it is a real null rather than a steering-damage artifact.

## What this means for the paper

W3 does **not** convert the representation-behaviour correlation into a causal claim, and the honest reading is that it strengthens the existing one. The project's three nulls (RSA convergence, item-level, model-level) said the readable intent representation is not used. The intervention now says the same thing with a manipulation rather than a correlation, and with a positive control that rules out the obvious alternative explanation: the same steering apparatus, at the same coefficients, in the same models, *does* move the moral contrast when it pushes the outcome direction. Intent is represented, linearly readable, and causally inert for this judgment.

Two limits to state with it. The intervention adds a fixed vector at one layer for all token positions; a more targeted intervention (per-position, multi-layer, or fitted on `belief_last` rather than the scoring prompt's final token) could still find an effect, so this bounds crude linear steering rather than all causal involvement. And the intent and outcome diff-of-means directions are not orthogonal (cos OLMo-2-1124-7B-Instruct 0.3894, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct 0.319), so part of what the intent diff-of-means direction does may be outcome leakage — which is another reason the probe-weight null is the cleaner evidence.
