# Checkpoint dissection: does instruction tuning create the outcome bias? (Roadmap #4)

**Question.** The core finding of this project is that instruct/chat models weight
*outcome* (harm caused) more than *intent* (harm attempted) relative to a human
developmental baseline. Is this bias introduced by a specific stage of the post-training
pipeline (SFT vs. preference optimization / DPO vs. RLHF), or is it a general effect of
alignment tuning regardless of *which* stage does it?

**Answer, in one line:** SFT alone is sufficient in every family tested, but the stage that
contributes most of the shift is recipe-dependent — see the revised interpretation below.
An earlier version of this writeup claimed the effect was localized to SFT and not
attributable to RLHF/DPO; that claim is withdrawn.

**Method.** For each model family, score every publicly released checkpoint along its
tuning pipeline (base → SFT → DPO/RLHF → final instruct/chat) with the same logprob-EV
protocol used elsewhere in this project, on the same 298-item intent×outcome set. For each
checkpoint, fit the 2×2 (intent × outcome) regression and record the **contrast**
(attempted − accidental) plus the full decomposition: `b_intent`, `b_outcome`,
`b_interaction`. Source: `outputs/experiments/checkpoint_dissection.csv` (code:
`code/experiments/16_checkpoint_dissection.py`).

## Interpretation — SFT is sufficient everywhere, but the locus is recipe-dependent

**The claim this section used to make was wrong, and the rescore is what corrects it.**
The earlier version reported the shift as localized to SFT and explicitly "not RLHF/DPO".
With Zephyr recovered and all three families on the 7-template basis, that no longer
survives: Zephyr is a direct counterexample. Per-family stage shares (each stage's step as
a fraction of that family's total base→final shift; generated by
`47_checkpoint_stage_shares.py`, full table in `CHECKPOINT_STAGE_SHARES.md`):

| Family | base→SFT | SFT→DPO | DPO→final | SFT share | shift concentrates at |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| OLMo-2-7B | −0.5879 | −0.0656 | −0.0350 | **85.4%** | SFT |
| Tülu-3-8B | −0.2651 | −0.1370 | −0.0030 (RLVR) | **65.4%** | SFT |
| Zephyr-7B | −0.1487 | −0.4020 | — (DPO is final) | **27.0%** | **DPO (73.0%)** |

**What the data support:**

1. **SFT alone is sufficient to induce outcome bias in all three families.** Every base
   checkpoint sits at essentially zero contrast (+0.0017, +0.0074, −0.0009) with
   near-zero `b_intent` and `b_outcome`, and a single stage of plain supervised
   fine-tuning — no preference optimization of any kind — moves every one of them off
   that neutral starting point, by **0.15 to 0.59** in contrast (Zephyr 0.1487, Tülu
   0.2651, OLMo 0.5879). This is the robust, three-for-three result.
2. **The relative contribution of SFT versus preference optimization is
   recipe-dependent.** SFT accounts for 85% of OLMo-2's total shift and 65% of Tülu-3's,
   but only 27% of Zephyr's — Zephyr's shift is concentrated at DPO (73%), where a single
   preference-optimization stage moves the contrast from −0.150 to −0.552. So the pipeline
   position where most of the drift accumulates is a property of the particular recipe,
   not a general fact about alignment tuning. **Do not describe the effect as localized to
   SFT, and do not claim it is "not RLHF/DPO" — Zephyr refutes both.**
3. **`b_outcome` grows several-fold faster than `b_intent`, in every family at every
   post-base stage — but the multiplier is a range, not a figure.** On the 7-template
   basis the final-stage ratios are OLMo 6.6×, Tülu 3.4×, Zephyr 7.7×, and across all
   non-base stages the ratio spans **3.0–7.7×**. The qualitative claim (outcome
   sensitivity rises several times faster than intent sensitivity, every family, every
   stage) is what is robust; the specific multiplier varies by more than a factor of two
   across recipes. The previously reported "2.5–3.9×" came from pre-rescore
   single-template fits and is retired. Base stages are excluded because both
   coefficients are ~0 there, making the quotient uninterpretable.
4. **All three bases are now engaged, which strengthens the causal reading.** This was a
   stated limitation and it is now resolved. Tülu-3's base was previously degenerate
   (`rating_std` 0.018) and is now **0.0966**; Zephyr's base, previously all zeros from
   the digit-token collapse, is now 0.0285; OLMo-2's is 0.0575. Every family therefore
   contrasts a *responsive* base that happens to be near zero on the intent/outcome
   asymmetry against its tuned descendants, rather than contrasting a non-responder
   against a responder. The base→tuned comparison is now like-for-like, so the near-zero
   base contrast reflects genuine indifference to the intent/outcome manipulation rather
   than a failure to engage with the rating task at all.
5. **Zephyr is no longer excluded.** It was previously dropped as degenerate at every
   stage (contrast and all coefficients exactly 0.000) on the strength of a scoring bug,
   not model behaviour. It is now a third supporting family for the sufficiency claim in
   (1) and the decisive counterexample for the locus claim in (2).

## Note on the Tülu-3-8B base checkpoint substitution

`meta-llama/Llama-3.1-8B`'s HF gate access request was submitted and still showed "awaiting
review from the repo authors" after more than 24 hours. To avoid blocking this analysis
indefinitely on a third-party approval queue, the base checkpoint was scored using
`unsloth/Meta-Llama-3.1-8B` instead — a widely-used, ungated re-upload of the identical
official weights (verified: same `LlamaForCausalLM` architecture/config, same
`_name_or_path` provenance metadata, downloadable without a gate). This is standard
practice in the open-weights community for exactly this situation and should not affect the
scoring in any way, since only the weights and tokenizer are used (no license-gated
metadata). If official gate access clears later, this checkpoint can be re-scored with
`meta-llama/Llama-3.1-8B` directly as a sanity check via
`python code/experiments/16_checkpoint_dissection.py --models Tulu-3-8B --force`.

## Extension to all open families (mini-dissection, `18_mini_dissection.py`)

**Honest resolution limit.** Only **OLMo-2, Tülu-3 and Zephyr** publish the intermediate
pipeline checkpoints (base → SFT → DPO → …) needed to resolve *where along the pipeline*
the shift accumulates.
**Qwen, Gemma, Mistral and Llama release only `base` and `instruct`** — no SFT/DPO
checkpoints exist on the Hub, so for those families we can only measure the **2-point
base→instruct** delta, not the per-stage trajectory. We do **not** fabricate intermediate
checkpoints. The combined figure (`mini_dissection.png`) draws the three full pipelines as
solid multi-stage lines and the other families as dashed base→instruct segments (middle
stages explicitly marked unresolved). All contrasts here use the single `human_verbatim`
template with logprob-EV, identical to the full-pipeline method above, with a paired
scenario bootstrap CI on each delta (`mini_dissection.csv`).

**Are the 2-point deltas consistent with the tuning-induced shift?** Mostly yes — with one
informative exception. Note these deltas cannot speak to the SFT-versus-DPO question at
all, since they collapse the whole pipeline into a single step:

| Family | base→instruct Δcontrast | 95% CI | Δb_outcome | consistent? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Qwen2.5-0.5B | −0.103 | [−0.123, −0.084] | +0.135 | yes |
| Qwen2.5-1.5B | −0.172 | [−0.212, −0.132] | +0.375 | yes |
| Qwen2.5-3B | −0.111 | [−0.173, −0.052] | +0.338 | yes |
| Qwen2.5-7B | −0.400 | [−0.465, −0.336] | +0.550 | yes |
| Qwen2.5-14B | −0.561 | [−0.636, −0.483] | +0.765 | yes |
| Gemma-2-9B | −0.251 | [−0.312, −0.191] | +0.485 | yes |
| Mistral-7B-v0.3 | 0.000 | [0.000, 0.000] | 0.000 | degenerate (excluded) |
| **Llama-3.1-8B** | **+0.126** | **[+0.079, +0.178]** | **−0.003** | **NO (exception)** |

1. **Sign consistency (6/7 non-degenerate families).** Every Qwen size and Gemma-2-9B shift
   in the **same negative direction** as the full pipelines' base→instruct endpoints, and in
   every case the shift is driven by `b_outcome` rising far more than `b_intent` — the
   identical mechanistic signature seen stage-by-stage in the full pipelines. So the
   tuning-induced outcome-bias shift is **not an artifact of the three families that happen
   to publish checkpoints**; it reproduces across Qwen and Gemma too.
2. **Rough magnitude.** The instruct-endpoint deltas span roughly −0.10 to −0.56. For Qwen
   the magnitude **scales with model size** (0.5B −0.10 → 14B −0.56), consistent with the
   scoring-parity finding that larger instruct models express the effect more strongly.
   These deltas are on the single-template basis and are not directly comparable in
   magnitude to the 7-template full-pipeline endpoints in the table above.
3. **The Llama-3.1-8B exception is real and worth reporting.** Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct is the
   only non-degenerate family that moves the *other* way (Δ = +0.126, toward intent) with
   `b_outcome` essentially flat. Its base sits near zero (−0.042) like the others, but its
   instruct checkpoint does **not** acquire the outcome bias. (The instruct weights are the
   ungated `unsloth/Meta-Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct` re-upload of Meta's official release, so this
   is a property of the model, not a mirror artifact; the master-ladder 7-template score
   agrees, placing it at ≈0.) This is a genuine counterexample to "instruction tuning always
   induces the outcome bias," and it means the effect — while robust across most families —
   is **recipe-dependent, not universal**. Llama-3.1's post-training (heavy RLHF with a
   different data mix) evidently does not push toward outcome-weighting the way the Tülu/OLMo/
   Qwen/Gemma recipes do.

## Bottom line

**Instruction/alignment tuning robustly and disproportionately increases outcome-weighting
relative to intent-weighting, and one stage of plain SFT is sufficient to induce it from a
neutral base in all three families with published checkpoints (drops of 0.15–0.59). But
which stage contributes most is recipe-dependent: SFT accounts for 85% of the shift in
OLMo-2 and 65% in Tülu-3, and only 27% in Zephyr, whose shift is 73% at DPO.** The
mechanism is always the same — `b_outcome` rises several times faster than `b_intent`,
3.0–7.7× across families and stages — and it reproduces across 6 of 7 non-degenerate open
families in the 2-point mini-dissection (Qwen ladder plus Gemma-2-9B). Three caveats keep
the claim defensible: (a) only three families publish the intermediate checkpoints, so the
stage-level story rests on OLMo-2 + Tülu-3 + Zephyr; (b) **Zephyr shows preference
optimization can carry most of the shift**, so no claim that the effect is localized to SFT
or absent from RLHF/DPO is supportable; and (c) **Llama-3.1-8B is a clear exception** whose
instruct checkpoint does not acquire the bias at all, showing the effect is a
common-but-not-universal, recipe-dependent property of instruction tuning rather than an
inevitability.
